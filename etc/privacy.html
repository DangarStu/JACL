JACL is interactive fiction created by Stuart Allen: the JACL app for iPad and this website (jacl.dangarmarine.com.au). OFFSIDE (offside.dangarmarine.com.au), a site for running social football competitions, is built with JACL and runs on the same server. Each is independent — this page explains what each one does and does not do with your information. In short: the app collects nothing, the website keeps only the minimum needed to run, and OFFSIDE keeps what a competition needs to publish its fixtures and results.

The JACL app for iPad

The app collects no personal information whatsoever, and it has no accounts or sign-in of any kind. It does not use Sign in with Google, Sign in with Apple, or any other login service — there is simply nothing to sign in to. It shows no advertising, contains no analytics or tracking, and makes no network connections. Everything happens on your iPad:

  • The games you play, the games you import, and your saved games are stored only on your device.
  • Nothing you type or do in the app is transmitted anywhere or seen by us.
  • Files you open in the app stay on your device.

Because the app collects no data, there is nothing for us to share, sell, or lose. You can download and play games entirely offline, without ever visiting the website.

This website

The website is separate from the app and is not required to use it. When you visit jacl.dangarmarine.com.au:

  • Browsing (these pages, the Guide, game downloads): like almost every website, the server keeps standard access logs — your IP address, browser type, and the pages or files requested, with a timestamp. These are used only to operate and secure the site. They are not used for advertising and are not sold or shared.
  • Playing a game in your browser: the game runs on the server, which may keep a temporary saved-game file for your session so you can pick up where you left off.
  • Optional Google Sign-In (website only): some games on the website let you sign in with Google so your browser saved-games are remembered between visits. This is a feature of the website's online player only — it is not part of the iPad app. If, and only if, you choose to sign in, Google provides the site with your basic account identifier so it can recognise you, and the site stores a jacl_session cookie in your browser to keep you signed in. It is used solely to associate your saved games with you. You can sign out at any time to clear it, and you can always play without signing in.

The website uses no third-party analytics or advertising trackers.

OFFSIDE

OFFSIDE (offside.dangarmarine.com.au) runs fixtures, results and league tables for social football competitions. Anyone can look at a competition without an account. Only a competition's organisers sign in.

What everyone can see. For each competition, OFFSIDE publishes what its organisers enter:

  • the competition's name and season, its teams, fixtures, results, forfeits and the league table;
  • who runs it: the names of its organisers, as given when they were added, and the name its creator chose when setting it up, along with a contact note the organisers write (such as which group chat to message) so players know who to ask;
  • each team's squad: players' names as the organisers enter them (usually first names), and optionally a position, shirt number and gender (female or male; it can be left as not stated). (see Hiding your name below);
  • who scored each goal, if recorded, and the top scorers.

Hiding your name. If you'd rather your name wasn't public, ask your competition's organisers to tick Hide name from the public on your entry. From then on, everyone except that competition's organisers (and the site's administrators) sees only the first and last letters of your name — “Shona” becomes “S…a” — in the squad list, under the results you score in, and in the top scorers table. The shortened form is made on the server, so your full name is never sent to their browser at all. Organisers still see your full name, because they need it to record results. Bear in mind that this hides your name, not you: your team, position, shirt number, gender and goals still appear, so people who know the team may still work out who you are. If you'd prefer not to be listed at all, ask to be removed instead.

What only organisers can see. A record of who entered or changed each result and when, with that organiser's name; the list of a competition's organisers; and the links used to invite new ones (each works once and expires after 7 days).

Signing in. Organisers sign in with Google. Google sends OFFSIDE a sign-in token that includes your name and email address, but OFFSIDE keeps only your Google account identifier (a number) to recognise you, plus the name you were given when you were made an organiser. It does not store your email address. A jacl_session cookie keeps you signed in until you sign out or it expires.

Cookies and storage in your browser. A user_id cookie lets the site remember which competition and tab you were looking at; the server keeps a small session file for it. Your browser also remembers which competition you last looked at, so OFFSIDE can reopen it, and on phones that you've seen the “add to home screen” tip so it isn't shown again. Both stay in your browser; they aren't sent to the server. Every page loads Google's sign-in button from Google, so Google receives the usual information a browser sends when loading it (such as your IP address); see Google's privacy policy.

Where it's kept. The data is stored on the Dangar Marine server, and a copy is backed up each night to a computer kept by Stuart Allen; backups are kept for 30 days. Like the rest of the website, the server keeps standard access logs. Nothing is used for advertising, sold or shared, and there are no analytics or trackers.

Corrections and removal. If you're listed in a squad and want your details changed, your name hidden, or your entry removed, ask your competition's organisers — they're named at the bottom of each page, with how to reach them — who can edit or delete players, or email the address below. Deleting a competition deletes its teams, players, results, scorers and change history.

Children

Neither the app nor the website is directed at children, and the app collects no information from anyone, children included.
